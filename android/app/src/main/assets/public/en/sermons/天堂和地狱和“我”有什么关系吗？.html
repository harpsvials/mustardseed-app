Introduction: The Question About Hell

Sometimes I hear people say, "Christians say God is a God of love, so why do those who do not believe in Him have to suffer in hell?" And the more they talk about it, the more dissatisfied they become with the Christian faith, and the more unwilling they are to accept the faith of Christ.

Valley of Hinnom — Where "Hell" Comes From

The word "hell" comes from a place name in the Old Testament, the "Valley of Hinnom." The Old Testament records that Israel imitated the customs of the Canaanites in this place, burning incense to foreign gods and even sacrificing their own children as burnt offerings to false gods.

Scripture · 2 Chronicles 28:3

He burned incense in the Valley of Hinnom and sacrificed his children in the fire, engaging in the detestable practices of the nations the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.

Scripture · Jeremiah 32:35

They built high places for Baal in the Valley of Hinnom to sacrifice their sons and daughters to Molek, though I never commanded—nor did it enter my mind—that they should do such a detestable thing and so make Judah sin.

Scripture · Matthew 5:22

But I tell you that anyone who is angry with a brother or sister will be subject to judgment. Again, anyone who says to a brother or sister, "Raca," is answerable to the court. And anyone who says, "You fool!" will be in danger of the fire of hell.

The "hell" mentioned here is, in the original language, the "Valley of Hinnom." Most of the time when the New Testament speaks of hell, it uses this word, "Valley of Hinnom."

Tartarus — Where Fallen Angels Are Held

Scripture · 2 Peter 2:4

For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but sent them to hell, putting them in chains of darkness to be held for judgment...

The hell Peter speaks of here comes from "Tartaros." The Greeks considered it a place deeper and lower than Hades. Peter says that after the angels sinned, they were cast into this place. So what is Hades?

Hades — A Place of Temporary Suffering

Scripture · Luke 16:23

In Hades, where he was in torment, he looked up and saw Abraham far away, with Lazarus by his side.

In the account of the rich man and the beggar Lazarus, the place where the rich man who did not follow God suffers temporarily after death is called "Hades." The place where Lazarus was is Paradise, the place where all who are saved go after death.

Scripture · Luke 23:43

Jesus answered him, "Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise."

Let us sort out the words about hell and Hades we have just mentioned. One is what we just spoke of, "Hades." Another is "Hell — Tartarus," the place where angels who sinned are imprisoned. The last is "Hell — Valley of Hinnom."

Lake of Fire — The Second Death

Scripture · Revelation 20:10

And the devil, who deceived them, was thrown into the lake of burning sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet had been thrown. They will be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

Scripture · Revelation 20:13

The sea gave up the dead that were in it, and death and Hades gave up the dead that were in them, and each person was judged according to what they had done.

Scripture · Revelation 20:14

Then death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. The lake of fire is the second death.

Scripture · Revelation 20:15

Anyone whose name was not found written in the book of life was thrown into the lake of fire.

Hades and death will be cast into the lake of fire and burned. The devil, the beast, the false prophet, and everyone whose name is not written in the book of life will be cast into the lake of fire. The Bible calls this the second death.

Paradise — The Dwelling of the Righteous

Now let us talk about those whose names are written in the book of life. According to the Bible, after they leave this world, they first go to Paradise.

Scripture · Luke 23:43

Jesus answered him, "Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise."

Scripture · 2 Corinthians 12:4

was caught up to paradise and heard inexpressible things, things that no one is permitted to tell.

Scripture · Revelation 2:7

Whoever has ears, let them hear what the Spirit says to the churches. To the one who is victorious, I will give the right to eat from the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God.

"Paradise" comes from ancient Persian, meaning "a walled royal garden."

Kingdom of God — Heaven You Can Experience Now

Another word related to "those justified by God" is heaven. In the Bible, heaven is "the heavens"; there are also two other words, "the kingdom of heaven" and "the kingdom of God."

Let us look at the heaven that Christians can experience now, and the heaven that is yet to come.

Scripture · Luke 17:21

Nor will people say, "Look, here it is," or "There it is," because the kingdom of God is within you.

The "kingdom" here in "the kingdom of God" is the same word as in "the kingdom of heaven." "Kingdom of heaven" and "kingdom of God" emphasize "the realm where God reigns" and "the heavenly realm." When you enter heaven, it means entering the heavenly realm where God reigns.

The Bible says the kingdom of God is within you. You must live in heaven now, not wait until after death to live in heaven.

Living in Heaven Right Now

The heaven we will enter in the future is, for us, "like a physical reality." The heaven now is, for us, "like a spiritual heaven." But if the righteous, after leaving this world, exist as spiritual beings and have to live in a physical heaven, that sounds difficult.

If you live in the physical world without experiencing the spiritual heaven, it will be hard for your spiritual existence after death to experience the physical heaven. When you begin to experience the spiritual heaven while living in the physical world, your spiritual existence after death will be able to experience the physical heaven.

In a real sense, heaven and those who will rise again transcend both the physical and the spiritual worlds. So while we live in the physical world, we need to live in heaven, to live in God's love.

Scripture · 1 John 4:8

Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love.

Scripture · 1 John 4:16

And so we know and rely on the love God has for us. God is love. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and God in them.

Scripture · 1 John 4:17

This is how love is made complete among us so that we will have confidence on the day of judgment: In this world we are like Jesus.

The Bible says God is love. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and God lives in the one who lives in love. Such a person will not fear the fire of hell at the judgment.

So now we need to live in love, to live in Christ, and by Christ to treat one another with love. When we love one another, people can begin to know God in their understanding.

Scripture · Colossians 2:2

My goal is that they may be encouraged in heart and united in love, so that they may have the full riches of complete understanding, in order that they may know the mystery of God, namely, Christ.

A Foretaste of Hell

Do people in the world today also get a foretaste of hell?

Dishes lovingly prepared by parents, in the eyes of many children, are not as good as getting pocket money to buy fried noodles or fried rice from a street stall. When a child cannot feel the parents' love, what he seeks is the freedom he imagines.

For children who reject their parents, the "terrible cooking" they talk about and their parents' management of them—to put it strongly—feel like hell to them.

We human beings are like God's children. Like rebellious children, we say God is domineering: why do those who do not believe in Him suffer in hell? Is He still a God of love?

It is not that God wants people to suffer in hell. Rather, rejecting God's love, people are in great pain! The biblical writers only used words people can understand to describe this condition of being without God.

Rejecting God's love makes people hate God. The pain in the life of one who rejects love is like a foretaste of hell. When we live in hatred, unwilling to forgive, we too are in great pain. When we are bitter and unforgiving, we find that everyone around us seems to be "bad-smelling, ugly-faced, and stepping on our feet."

When we are willing to draw near to God's word and live the life God wants for us, we begin to live in God's love, like heaven itself. And I firmly believe that those who live in love on earth will one day live in the place where God reigns in heaven—a kingdom full of love! Amen!

Think · Questions

Let's Think Together

1. Using the parable of the rebellious child rejecting his parents' love, how can we help people understand the difficulty that "God is loving, yet those who reject God fall into pain/hell"?

2. After understanding the differences between the "Valley of Hinnom," "Tartarus," "Hades," "Paradise," and the "Lake of Fire," what new clarity does this bring to your understanding of judgment after death and God's salvation?

3. Jesus said "the kingdom of God is within you." How can you truly experience the peace of God's reign and the reality of heaven in daily life or in mutual love within the church?

4. Living in hatred and bitterness is like "a foretaste of hell." Facing hurt in relationships, how can we take the first step of forgiveness through the love of Christ?
