One Body of Water, Many Names

The “sea” we speak of is the “Sea of Galilee” in the Bible. Because the writers had different backgrounds, eras and geographical names, it appears under different names in Scripture.

Named after the region of Galilee, it is called the Sea of Galilee; named after the plain and town on its western shore, it is called the Lake of Gennesaret; and later it was named after the Romanized metropolis built on its shore by a Roman tetrarch — the Sea of Tiberias.

So you will find that when Jesus was by the sea or the lake in the Bible, it was always the same body of water — strictly speaking, a freshwater lake.

This body of water lies in the Jordan Valley rift, the extension of the East African Rift. It is a low-altitude basin surrounded by mountains, like a deep bowl set among the hills.

Because of its low altitude, the air over the lake heats and expands quickly, forming a low-pressure zone; when cold high-altitude air meets the warm low-pressure air over the lake, it plunges sharply downward. This is why sudden storms appear in the Bible, capable of raising waves two to three meters high within minutes. This is also the backdrop of the event when Jesus walked on the sea.

By the Lake of Gennesaret

Let us read Luke 5:1–11. By the Lake of Gennesaret the nets nearly tore, and the boats nearly sank from the great catch.

Scripture · Luke 5:1–2

One day as Jesus was standing by the Lake of Gennesaret, the people were crowding around him and listening to the word of God. He saw at the water’s edge two boats, left there by the fishermen, who were washing their nets.

Scripture · Luke 5:3–5

He got into one of the boats, the one belonging to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from shore. Then he sat down and taught the people from the boat. When he had finished speaking, he said to Simon, “Put out into deep water, and let down the nets for a catch.” Simon answered, “Master, we’ve worked hard all night and haven’t caught anything. But because you say so, I will let down the nets.”

Scripture · Luke 5:6–7

When they had done so, they caught such a large number of fish that their nets began to break. So they signaled their partners in the other boat to come and help them, and they came and filled both boats so full that they began to sink.

Scripture · Luke 5:8–9

When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus’ knees and said, “Go away from me, Lord; I am a sinful man!” For he and all his companions were astonished at the catch of fish they had taken.

Scripture · Luke 5:10–11

And so were James and John, the sons of Zebedee, Simon’s partners. Then Jesus said to Simon, “Don’t be afraid; from now on you will fish for people.” So they pulled their boats up on shore, left everything and followed him.

Another Record of the Call: By the Sea of Galilee

Scripture · Mark 1:16–18

As Jesus walked beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and his brother Andrew casting a net into the lake, for they were fishermen. “Come, follow me,” Jesus said, “and I will send you out to fish for people.” At once they left their nets and followed him.

Scripture · Mark 1:19–20

When he had gone a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John in a boat, preparing their nets. Without delay he called them, and they left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and followed him.

A Meditation of Imagination: Peter Who Toiled All Night

As we said earlier, the “shore of the Lake of Gennesaret” in Luke and the “Sea of Galilee” in Mark are the same body of water. With this background, you can easily see that the calling of Simon Peter and Andrew, and of James and John, happened at the same time — different people recorded the same event differently.

Allow me to use my imagination to understand this passage. The Scriptures record that before Jesus called the twelve apostles he spent a whole night in prayer, and that he often prayed at night before serving the next day. So I believe that when Jesus called Peter, James and John — the three closest to him among the disciples — he must also have prayed through the night.

Peter had toiled all night fishing and caught nothing. In that state, the crowd pressed around Jesus to hear the word of God while Peter washed his nets. Jesus got into Peter’s boat, asked him to push out from shore, and taught the crowds.

Peter, who had worked all night and gained nothing, still offered his boat for Jesus to preach from. Put yourself in his place: you have worked hard and you are exhausted, and yet someone asks you to come serve at church — and even more, to use your boat.

Without a spirit of offering and sacrifice, that would be hard to do. When things go well, giving some time and money is easier; but when we have labored all night and gained nothing, body and spirit exhausted — if staying up had brought a full harvest, it would be easier to stay excited.

But at this moment Peter was clearly worn out. If you were in that state, would you still offer yourself to Jesus?

In this state, Peter served Jesus by pushing the boat and listening to his teaching. But when Jesus finished, you recall that Peter had just washed his nets. Jesus — a carpenter, yet a master fisherman — actually asked the weary professional fisherman Peter to put out into deep water and let down the nets.

The one thing a person exhausted from a night’s work wants most is sleep. He had already served for two hours, grudgingly, and was tired enough. Yet Jesus asked him to cast his own nets — did he not know they had just been washed clean? But Peter said, “Master, we’ve worked hard all night and haven’t caught anything. But because you say so, I will let down the nets.”

When Peter let down the nets, he caught so many fish that the nets began to break, and he had to call the men in the other boat to help. The catch was so great the boats were about to sink — yet God arranged it just in time, so they did not sink, and even both boats were filled. That catch was already the very limit those boats could carry. Notice that James and John were watching nearby, jaws dropping. (Luke 5:5–10)

When Peter saw the great catch, he fell at Jesus’ feet and said, “Lord, go away from me, for I am a sinful man!” Peter was an unschooled ordinary man (Acts 4:13), yet he knew better than anyone: I am a sinner, and Jesus is Lord!

In the film, Peter asks, “How did you catch so many fish?” Jesus says to Peter, “Follow me!” Peter asks, “What can I do?” Jesus answers, “Change the world!” It was in such circumstances that Peter followed Jesus — and so did James, John and Andrew.

By the Sea of Tiberias

Let us read John 21:2–13.

Scripture · John 21:2–4

Simon Peter, Thomas (also known as Didymus), Nathanael from Cana in Galilee, the sons of Zebedee, and two other disciples were together. “I’m going out to fish,” Simon Peter told them, and they said, “We’ll go with you.” So they went out and got into the boat, but that night they caught nothing. Early in the morning, Jesus stood on the shore, but the disciples did not realize that it was Jesus.

Scripture · John 21:5–7

He called out to them, “Friends, haven’t you any fish?” “No,” they answered. He said, “Throw your net on the right side of the boat and you will find some.” When they did, they were unable to haul the net in because of the large number of fish. Then the disciple whom Jesus loved said to Peter, “It is the Lord!” As soon as Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he wrapped his outer garment around him (for he had taken it off) and jumped into the water.

Scripture · John 21:8–9

The other disciples followed in the boat, towing the net full of fish, for they were not far from shore, about a hundred yards. When they landed, they saw a fire of burning coals there with fish on it, and some bread.

Scripture · John 21:10–13

Jesus said to them, “Bring some of the fish you have just caught.” So Simon Peter climbed back into the boat and dragged the net ashore. It was full of large fish, 153, but even with so many the net was not torn. Jesus said to them, “Come and have breakfast.” None of the disciples dared ask him, “Who are you?” They knew it was the Lord. Jesus came, took the bread and gave it to them, and did the same with the fish.

The Moment Memories Overlap

John 21:2 says the two sons of Zebedee were there with Peter. Who were they? From Mark 1:19 we know they were James and John.

Scripture · Mark 1:19

When he had gone a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John in a boat, preparing their nets.

After Jesus’ suffering, the Scripture says, the disciples were scattered. Peter was together with James, John, Nathanael, Thomas, and two other disciples whose names we do not know.

Here were doubting Thomas, Peter who denied the Lord three times, and John, the one whom the Lord loved. If you believe you are the one the Lord loves — the one closest to him, like John — you can also come to the net with weak brothers and sisters, and perhaps in fellowship you may help the weak meet the Lord.

Peter said, “I’m going out to fish.” They said, “We’ll go with you.” Companionship is the best comfort we can give people! And Jesus was actually not far from them. If you feel weak, like Peter, know that Jesus is not far from you — he loves Peter, and he loves you.

Jesus said, “Friends, haven’t you any fish?” These fishermen had again toiled all night and caught nothing. Jesus said, “Throw your net on the right side of the boat.” When they cast the net, they could not haul it in because of the abundance of fish.

Imagine that you are one of those two unnamed disciples. Last time you saw Jesus teach Peter to fish; this time, again empty-handed, someone else teaches Peter and these fishermen how to fish! Suddenly, goosebumps rise all over you! Sacred presence and a tide of memory flood in! It dawns on you — could this be the Lord?

All of you know in your hearts that it is the Lord. John, quick of step, blurts out, “It is the Lord!” You are about to cry! Tears flow! The moment Peter hears the Lord’s voice, he grabs his clothes and jumps into the sea! You watch Peter swim desperately toward Jesus, and at the same time you hear yourself wailing and weeping!

You and I are so weak — sometimes we dare not even confess the name of Jesus. Yet Jesus loves you and loves me, died on the cross for us, and after his resurrection came to find us and bring us home!

Peter no longer said, “Lord, I am a sinner.” Instead, in a frenzy of tears he ran toward Jesus, and the other disciples went to the Lord with a full catch! This time what they saw was not fish, but Jesus. Jesus no longer needed to say, “Follow me!” The last time, Jesus was arrested and the disciples scattered.

This time the disciples would even die with the Lord. Peter preached the gospel and was crucified upside down; Thomas preached in India and was martyred; most of the other disciples were also martyred for the gospel of Christ; the one the Lord loved was exiled to a desolate island.

Jesus loves you just as he loved these disciples. Are you willing to put down your nets and return to the Lord’s side to follow him?

Reflection

When you find yourself weary and weak, like Peter who toiled all night and caught nothing, are you still willing to push the boat out for Jesus? Tell us how you feel.

When you think of Jesus teaching Peter and the others to fish twice, as the two similar events overlap in your memory, what stirs in your heart?
