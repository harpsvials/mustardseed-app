What Does Heaven and Hell Have to Do with Me?

God is love. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and God in them1 John 4:16

Starting from the question "God is loving, so why do those who do not believe in Him suffer in hell?": sorting out the Bible's words about hell and Hades — the Valley of Hinnom (the origin of "hell" in the New Testament), Tartarus (where fallen angels are held), Hades (where the unbelieving suffer temporarily after death), Paradise (the dwelling of the saved) and the Lake of Fire (the second death); then about heaven and the kingdom of God — the kingdom of God is within you, you must live in heaven now, living in God's love; finally, using the parable of the rebellious child who rejects his parents' love, showing that it is not God who makes people suffer, but rejecting God's love brings pain — living in hatred and bitterness is like a foretaste of hell. With 4 questions for reflection.

Sermon · 02

What Does It Mean to Believe in Jesus?

If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow meLuke 9:23

Beginning with Mark Twain's dry humor about reading the Bible—what truly troubled him was not what he could not understand, but what he understood all too well yet failed to live out. It compares the background of believing in Jesus in the early church with that of today: for the early disciples, believing in Jesus meant giving their lives to Jesus. Returning to Israel's history—from exile and return to the Aliyah—it explores why the religious leaders persecuted Jesus, and Judas's disappointment and betrayal, reminding believers today: it is not our circumstances that drive us from God, but whether the source of our faith is Jesus Christ. Believing in Jesus is not about money, but about whether we will give our lives—may we follow Jesus faithfully and become people who bear fruit a hundredfold.

Sermon · 03

Jesus Teaches Peter to Fish Twice

Put out into deep water, and let down the nets for a catchLuke 5:4

Beginning with the geography of the Sea of Galilee (the Lake of Gennesaret and the Sea of Tiberias are in fact the same body of water), this sermon compares the records of the call in Luke 5 and Mark 1. It meditates on Peter, who toiled all night and caught nothing, yet still obeyed, pushed out into deep water, received a net full of fish and fell at the Lord’s feet. Then we see the risen Jesus teaching Peter to fish again by the Sea of Tiberias; as the two similar events overlap in memory, the disciples recognize the Lord. Finally, two questions invite us to talk together: when you are weary and weak, are you still willing to push the boat out for Jesus? And what stirs in your heart when the two fishing events overlap in your memory?

Sermon · 04

God Hears Our Cry

Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and the door will be opened to youLuke 11:9

Beginning with the joy of answered prayer and the helplessness of unanswered prayer, this sermon shows us: clear the channel of prayer—sin, doubt, pleasure, pride and self-righteousness all block prayer; have faith in God's Word, for faith comes from God's Word; pray according to God's will; and keep praying—the widow and the unjust judge, Daniel's twenty-one days of prayer, P.U.S.H. (Pray Until Something Happens). Finally it asks us to reflect: is the channel of my prayer clear, am I continuing to wait in prayer that agrees with God's will, and do I believe God will hear?

Sermon · 05

Forgiveness Sets You Free

Forgive, and you will be forgiven. (The Greek word here means “released.”)Luke 6:37

Starting from an unpleasant incident on the way to send a child to school, this sermon brings up the theme of forgiveness: do not judge or condemn others, lest in refusing to forgive you judge them; the butterfly effect of unforgiveness—bitterness and hatred devour life. How many times should we forgive? Not seven times, but seventy times seven. Imitate Jesus without ceasing, and consider how great the king’s forgiveness was; unforgiveness puts us in the hands of the jailer—choose forgiveness, and you will release yourself into freedom.

Sermon · 06

Healing and Deliverance

By his wounds you have been healed1 Peter 2:24

The speaker first shares his own firsthand experience of God’s healing, then shows that healing is the DNA of the church—every sick person who comes among us should be healed. Faith is powerful: believe the Bible’s promises about healing and deliverance; if you believe, you will see the glory of God, believe that you have received it, and it will be yours. It also reveals that disease comes from the thief in the spiritual realm, and closes with an altar call of decision prayer.

Seeing God’s Heart for Revival Through Healing

And these signs will accompany those who believeMark 16:17
