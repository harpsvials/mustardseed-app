Learning to Obey

If you love me, you will keep my commandments.John 14:15

This sermon begins with the speaker’s calling—moved by the Holy Spirit to become an intercessor for the revival and unity of the Zhuzhou church, she stepped forward on May 5 (the day of Lixia, the start of summer) to receive the pastor’s blessing. She looks back on her pride and rebellion before knowing God, and on coming to faith and being baptized in 2018. She then understands that obedience is not merely “doing what you are told”; it is the surrender of the inner will combined with action. Because of Christ’s love, we are willing to obey (John 14:15; 1 John 4:19; Joyce Meyer’s teaching). She shares how, at the Hong Kong Fire Conference in late September 2025, God asked her to do something she deeply did not want to do—she shut herself in her home for two days to pray, and when she was willing to obey, the burden lifted and she completed God’s will step by step. She then explains why obeying people is harder: pride makes no one willing to yield (Philippians 2:3); God wants us to obey the authorities He has established—pastors who keep watch over our souls as those who must give an account (Hebrews 13:17). She recalls her own resentment when the pastor’s wife corrected her, and the cracks in her life that allowed the enemy to damage unity. Finally, through the dream message “to serve, you must obey,” she shares how she learned humility, obedience, and honor for her pastors, and tasted the sweetness of being corrected. Obedience brings blessing! With 2 reflection questions.

Sermon · 02

Words That Build Faith

So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ.Romans 10:17

Faith is confidence in God. Starting from “faith comes from hearing” (Romans 10:17), this sermon unpacks the Greek words—pistis (faith), akoe (hearing), and rhema (the word God is speaking now)—and shares four ways to encounter God’s word in daily life, how to test what you receive, and Mary’s example of treasuring God’s revelation. It contrasts rhema and logos, showing that every word of God carries power; what matters is receiving, accepting, and obeying. It then teaches how to test what you receive—convictions that contradict the Bible are not faith, and expectation is not faith either. Finally, it presents whole-hearted faith as the union of rational understanding, emotional hope, and willing obedience. With 2 reflection questions.

Sermon · 03

Idolatry and Familial Curses

You shall not bow down to them or serve them, for I the LORD your God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and the fourth generation.Deuteronomy 5:9

Beginning with the apostles’ instruction to Gentile believers (to abstain from things polluted by idols, from sexual immorality, and from what has been strangled and from blood), this sermon explains the curses brought by making idols, bowing down to them, bringing idol-related objects into the home, and practicing sorcery and divination. It shows how idolatry opens a breach for evil spirits, becoming a generational curse and the transfer of family spirits. It examines breaches such as worshipping ancestral tablets, fortune-telling, and being dedicated as adopted children to false gods. Finally, it gives six stages for breaking family curses—removing devoted things, standing in the breach to confess the sins of ancestors, thoroughly forgiving, revoking agreements made with evil spirits, binding the strong man and commanding spirits to leave, and persevering in spiritual watchfulness in freedom. With 4 reflection questions.

What Does It Mean to Believe in Jesus?

If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow meLuke 9:23

Beginning with Mark Twain's dry humor about reading the Bible—what truly troubled him was not what he could not understand, but what he understood all too well yet failed to live out. It compares the background of believing in Jesus in the early church with that of today: for the early disciples, believing in Jesus meant giving their lives to Jesus. Sermon · 12

Rowing Against the Current, or Sailing With It?

Therefore we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away from it.希伯来书 2:1

Starting from the speaker’s own experience of twice losing weight and then regaining it, this sermon points out that a Christian’s spiritual life is like rowing against the current—if you do not press forward, you fall back. The strong hear and obey God’s word consistently; the weak hear and obey intermittently; the obese only hear but never obey. From Hebrews 2:1-4 we learn to pay much closer attention to what we have heard lest we drift away—to be “serious” means to focus on, pay attention to, and devote ourselves to God’s word. Hebrews 10:38-39 and Ezekiel 33:13-16 show that faith with works is living faith, that shrinking back leads to destruction, and that the wicked who turn and repent will surely live. Tracing the changes from the Reformation era to the worship styles of the Chinese church, we see that every generation follows God and practices His truth on the foundation of the previous generation. When Christians begin to follow God and enter the heart and current of God for this generation, they are sailing with the current—and their spiritual lives grow rapidly. Includes 2 discussion questions.

How Do We Pray to God and Receive an Answer?

And this is the confidence that we have toward him, that if we ask anything according to his will he hears us.约翰一书 5:14

This sermon begins with the seventy disciples returning joyfully after praying, healing the sick, and casting out demons in Jesus’ name—and contrasts them with the seven sons of Sceva in Acts, who invoked Jesus’ name presumptuously and were overpowered. Authority and power come from a life that follows Jesus. The core of faith is not methodology but life—whether you are serious, whether you are truly willing to follow Jesus, and whether you let faith reshape your way of living. From 1 John 5:14-15 and Mark 11:24, we learn that to ask “according to his will” we must know God and Jesus Christ His Son, and that being “confident” means having a good relationship of trust with God. James 4:2-3 shows that we do not receive because we do not ask, or because we ask wrongly. Matthew 5:33-37 clarifies that prayer is not wish-making or bartering, but truly trusting Jesus and praying according to God’s will. The sermon closes with a five-point summary and a shared passage from a one-on-one discipleship course.

Sermon · 14

We Need God’s Healing

I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk!使徒行传 3:6

Starting from the real needs of brothers and sisters in the church who suffer from back and leg pain, this sermon teaches that from the church’s perspective we should speak like Peter: “In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk (be healed).” It unfolds the background of the healing of the man lame from birth at the Beautiful Gate in Acts 3—the hour of prayer, the Nicanor Gate (the Beautiful Gate), the fifteen steps, and begging as a way of making a living. The word “asked” is not lowly, for Jesus and the high priest’s “questioning” of Jesus use the same word; you do not pray to beg Jesus for alms, but to speak with God according to His word. Peter “looked intently” at the lame man and said, “I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you”—and you have Jesus, so you can give Jesus to those who ask something of you. When the lame man was pulled up, his feet and ankles were immediately made strong: do not say “my illness is too serious,” but say “immediately made strong!” Your feelings run slower than your faith—the feeling of healing returns to your body through your faith. Finally, through Romans 10:10 and 1 Peter 2:24, we are encouraged to declare positive words with our mouths: by Jesus’ wounds we have been healed. Includes 3 reflection questions.

Sermon · 15

Believing and Focusing on the Word of God

All things are possible for one who believes.马可福音 9:23

Believing has power! From the centurion who believed one word healed his servant, to the friends who carried their paralyzed friend to Jesus by faith, the woman with the flow of blood healed by touching Jesus’ garment, blind Bartimaeus receiving sight through faith, and the Canaanite woman who won her daughter’s release by faith—what Jesus commended was the faith behind the actions. Faith as small as a mustard seed can move mountains; all things are possible for one who believes. The world says “seeing is believing,” but Jesus says blessed are those who believe without seeing. We then see how we habitually help people in the world’s way, and the questions that arise in our hearts during trials: faith is not born after the trial ends but in the process, when we choose to believe and follow God in the pain. Through the double-slit experiment (“observation changes the result”) and the Hawthorne effect (“being watched brings change”), we see that when we focus on God’s word and soak in it, His word releases power to heal us. Finally, the sermon shares the difference between active and passive following, how checking the body after prayer catalyzes and strengthens faith, and that in every trial and hardship we are not alone—Jesus loves you, you are not alone!
