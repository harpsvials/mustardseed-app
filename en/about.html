Vision

Build up the body of Christ and proclaim the gospel of the kingdom of God.

Mission

Relying on the word of God and the power of the Holy Spirit, build up the body of Christ; set the oppressed free, release the captives, and proclaim the good news of the kingdom with power.

Strategy

We emphasize a two-fold pastoral care through Sunday services and small groups: through fervent prayer, brothers and sisters together experience God’s presence, healing and deliverance, and in the small groups we accept one another and build loving relationships.

Statement of faith

Salvation

We believe that whoever truly receives Jesus Christ as the Son of God—who came into the world for our sins, was crucified for us on the cross, and rose from the dead on the third day—becomes a child of God and receives eternal life in his kingdom.

We believe that God keeps those who truly trust in him, preserving their faith so they never fall away into ruin; we also believe that those who are saved may still be weak and sin, but their hearts will produce a godly sorrow that leads to salvation, so that they repent and live a new life.

We believe that those who truly receive Jesus Christ are not the kind who fall away into ruin; we also believe that the church is not a place for debating theological ideas, but for continually encouraging one another in Christ’s love, closely following Jesus Christ, committing to the church, serving one another, and living out a new life of constant giving like Christ.

Baptism

We believe that believers should receive baptism. Baptism means that our old self has been crucified with Christ on the cross; it is no longer our old self that lives within us, but Christ living in us, so that we become a new creation and are raised with Christ.

Christ

Jesus Christ is fully man and fully God, the tangible and bodily revelation of the Triune God. He was crucified, raised, and ascended to heaven for our sins, bearing all our sins, so that those who believe in him may inherit eternal life.

The Trinity

The Triune God is one God, revealed in three persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. It is like water, which has three states (liquid water, steam, and ice): liquid water is neither steam nor ice; ice is neither liquid water nor steam; steam is neither liquid water nor ice; yet liquid water is a form of water, steam is a form of water, and ice is a form of water. Likewise in the three persons of God: the Father is not the Son, nor the Holy Spirit; the Son is not the Father, nor the Holy Spirit; the Holy Spirit is not the Father, nor the Son. Yet the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; and God is three in one—there is only one unique God.

Communion

The bread and cup of Communion represent our receiving of Jesus’ body, the wounds he suffered for us, and the blood he shed, so that we receive the price Jesus Christ paid and have eternal life.

We believe that Communion is a blessing from God for every brother and sister; every Christian who has truly repented should receive Communion; we believe that no reason should keep anyone from receiving the body and blood of Christ.

Sickness and healing

Sickness is not sent by God to make us blessed. Sickness is the work of the devil, but God can help us know in our illness that he is our Healer, and God can cause the sick members to begin to rely on him.

One of the reasons the Son of God came into the world was to destroy the devil’s work! Jesus wants to heal those who seek him.

Marriage

Scripture · Genesis 2:24

That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh.

Marriage should be a union of mutual love in Christ.

Divorce

We believe that a Christian’s marriage is a gift from God, but if a believer is already married to an unbeliever, the Christian should not leave the unbelieving spouse. Yet Scripture says that if the unbelieving partner insists on leaving, let them leave; the brother or sister is not bound in such circumstances.

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 7:15

But if the unbeliever leaves, let it be so. The brother or sister is not bound in such circumstances.

We should protect our marriage, but if an unbelieving spouse insists on divorce because of an affair or other reasons, Paul says the brother or sister should let the divorce happen, regaining the freedom of being single and no longer bound by the former marriage.

Remarriage

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 7:8–9

Now to the unmarried and the widows I say: it is good for them to stay unmarried, as I do. But if they cannot control themselves, they should marry, for it is better to marry than to burn with passion.

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 7:39

A woman is bound to her husband as long as he lives. But if her husband dies, she is free to marry anyone she wishes, but he must belong to the Lord.

For brothers and sisters who have already divorced, Scripture teaches that they may decide for themselves whether to marry; if they have physical desires, it is good to marry—but only someone in the Lord.

Things to abstain from

Scripture · Acts 15:28–29

It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us not to burden you with anything beyond the following requirements: you are to abstain from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from the meat of strangled animals and from sexual immorality. You will do well to avoid these things. Farewell.

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 5:11–13

But now I am writing to you that you must not associate with anyone who claims to be a brother or sister but is sexually immoral or greedy, an idolater or slanderer, a drunkard or swindler. Do not even eat with such people. What business is it of mine to judge those outside the church? Are you not to judge those inside? God will judge those outside. Expel the wicked person from among you.

From a spiritual perspective, food sacrificed to idols, blood, the meat of strangled animals, and sexual immorality bring ruling and bondage into our lives; therefore Scripture tells us to keep ourselves from doing these things.

Scripture also adds requirements for Christian conduct: it forbids greed, slander, drunkenness and swindling, and reaffirms the ban on sexual immorality and idolatry. Moreover, if a brother or sister refuses to heed the church’s teaching—that is, refuses to repent—they must be expelled from the church.

Why should someone be asked to leave our church?

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 5:9–13

I wrote to you in my letter not to associate with sexually immoral people—not at all meaning the people of this world who are immoral, or the greedy and swindlers, or idolaters. In that case you would have to leave this world. But now I am writing to you that you must not associate with anyone who claims to be a brother or sister but is sexually immoral or greedy, an idolater or slanderer, a drunkard or swindler. Do not even eat with such people. What business is it of mine to judge those outside the church? Are you not to judge those inside? God will judge those outside. Expel the wicked person from among you.

Repentance

If we sin only in our hearts, we only need to repent to God. But when our sin is exposed before others, we need to repent publicly before people and ask forgiveness from those we have hurt. In this way you receive God’s forgiveness, peace in your heart, and are reconciled with others, with God, and with yourself.

For Christians whom the church has asked not to attend services—until they have shown their repentance and been received back by the church—do not associate with them, not even eat with them; this is also the teaching of Scripture.

When they repent, we must show them steadfast and unwavering love.
