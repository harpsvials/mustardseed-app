Introduction

There are many kinds of prayer, but today we share about this: how do we pray so that we receive an answer? When we as Christians experience God answering our prayers, and we receive what we asked for, we are usually very happy and excited. In fact, the disciples in the Bible were also very excited when their prayers were answered.

Authority Comes from Following Jesus

Scripture · Luke 10:17-20

The seventy-two returned with joy, saying, “Lord, even the demons are subject to us in your name!” And he said to them, “I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven. Behold, I have given you authority to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall hurt you. Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”

Jesus told the disciples not to rejoice because the demons submitted to them, but to rejoice because their names are written in heaven. In this passage, Jesus sent the disciples ahead of Him to the places He was about to go, to first heal the sick and cast out demons in His name. When the disciples obeyed Jesus and served, they were excited to experience God’s power and ran back to report to Jesus.

Why did the disciples have the power to heal and cast out demons in their lives? It was because they obeyed and followed Jesus—only then did authority and power come out of their lives! Compare this with the few men in Acts who did not follow Jesus, who presumptuously invoked Jesus’ name to cast out demons and were overpowered by the demon-possessed man.

Scripture · Acts 19:13-16

Then some of the itinerant Jewish exorcists undertook to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits, saying, “I adjure you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims.” Seven sons of a Jewish high priest named Sceva were doing this. But the evil spirit answered them, “Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?” And the man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them, mastered all of them and overpowered them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

The methods and words of healing and deliverance are easy to learn, but authority and power are not easy to learn. When you truly follow Jesus with your life, you experience not only God’s grace, but authority in your life—you can invoke Jesus’ name, the sick are healed, and unclean spirits are driven out! Amen!

Of course, there is one thing we must remember: the sick being healed and unclean spirits being driven out both serve the preaching of Jesus. Healing and deliverance are not the goal—Jesus is the center of our lives.

The Core of Faith Is Life

Today’s theme is: “How do we pray to God and receive an answer?” If Jesus is the center of your life, you have already begun to enter. If you only want the gifts Jesus gives, or to experience God’s blessings in prayer, you are still lingering at the door.

Because the core of faith is not methodology—the core of faith is life. That is: are you serious? Are you willing to truly follow Jesus? Are you willing to let faith reshape (redefine) your way of living?

If you are willing to begin following God and make Jesus the center of your life, then the methods and practices shared below will become a blessing to your life.

When you are willing to follow Jesus with your life, methods help you practice and form habits. In the process of practicing God’s word, you begin to truly know God, and you become the pattern you have chosen to pursue.

Of course, if you are not willing to become a disciple who follows Jesus, all these methods and habits will one day become what you see as shackles and bondage, and you will want to break free.

Whether we are willing to follow Jesus will one day be revealed. As the saying goes, “A long road tests a horse’s strength.”

Scripture · 1 Corinthians 3:13

each one’s work will become manifest, for the Day will disclose it, because it will be revealed by fire, and the fire will test what sort of work each one has done.

Ask According to His Will

Now let us look at the method of prayer built on the foundation of life.

Scripture · 1 John 5:14-15

And this is the confidence that we have toward him, that if we ask anything according to his will he hears us. And if we know that he hears us in whatever we ask, we know that we have the requests that we have asked of him.

These are exciting and stirring verses—after all, who would not want prayer to “receive whatever we ask”? But the verses have a precondition: according to his will. Who is “he”? He is Jesus Christ, God incarnate. You need to know God and His Son Jesus Christ.

He is not the son of the carpenter Joseph; He is the Son of God, the miracle of the Word becoming flesh, born into this world through the Virgin Mary.

Your “miracle” or your need is to be conceived in your prayer. As Pastor David Yonggi Cho discusses in The Fourth Dimension, our needs are conceived in prayer through faith.

To continue: to ask according to the will of Jesus Christ the Son of God, you need to know what kind of God He is—not just the literal words, but through the text of the Bible, knowing God’s heart, understanding the Spirit’s meaning to interpret the essence of the text, and applying it to your own life. This is asking according to His will.

Bold Confidence and Trust

Besides asking according to His will, we also need to be “confident”—being confident means the relationship is good, and a good relationship also means “trust.”

Scripture · Mark 11:24

Therefore I tell you, whatever you ask in prayer, believe that you have received it, and it will be yours.

If we cannot place ourselves in the hands of Jesus Christ, it shows we do not trust Him enough—that we are not truly trusting Him, and the relationship is not as good as we think.

Faith that follows Jesus means believing that when we do what Jesus asks us to do, Jesus will cover for us. We cannot say we believe with our mouths while our lifestyle acts as if Jesus does not exist—relying only on ourselves, only on material things.

Different parents respond to their children’s needs in different ways. Some parents are more anxious and prepare everything for their children. Others are less anxious and only give when their children ask. God also responds to different prayers in different ways. For example, salvation—God has prepared it completely; when we receive it, we have it, and if we do not receive it, we do not. But healing and some outward needs usually require you to ask God, asking according to His will.

Not Asking and Asking Wrongly

Scripture · James 4:2-3

You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and quarrel. You do not have, because you do not ask. You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions.

If what you ask God for would become (or has already become) your idol, you will not receive it. Or if the grace you pray for comes from rivalry and comparison, according to the Bible you will not receive these things.

If you do not receive your basic needs, it is because you do not ask God, or you do not believe God will give them to you—you just mumble through prayer without believing God will give them. Then you will not receive them either.

Not asking and asking wrongly both end in not receiving. Only by asking with faith according to God’s will do we receive; and God will accomplish what we ask in ways beyond our imagination.

Everyone wants prayer to receive everything, but receiving everything is not wish-making, and God is not an Aladdin trapped in a lamp.

If any faith or religion claims it can unconditionally satisfy any of your needs, be careful—either it is deceiving you, or it is deceiving you.

Prayer Is Not Wish-Making

In the Old Testament, making a vow means swearing an oath. The Old Testament says if you make a vow you must fulfill it—meaning when you ask God to accomplish something, you promise to give something in return; when it is accomplished, you must fulfill your vow according to your promise.

But the New Testament says, “Do not take an oath at all.” Let us look together at Matthew 5:33-37.

Scripture · Matthew 5:33-37

“Again you have heard that it was said to those of old, ‘You shall not swear falsely, but shall perform to the Lord what you have sworn.’ But I say to you, Do not take an oath at all: either by heaven, for it is the throne of God, or by the earth, for it is his footstool, or by Jerusalem, for it is the city of the great King. And do not take an oath by your head, for you cannot make one hair white or black. Let what you say be simply ‘Yes’ or ‘No’; anything more than this comes from evil.

Also, the “wish-making” we talk about today is different from the vows in the Bible. Most of today’s wish-making resembles an Aladdin’s lamp. What I want to remind you is that our prayer should not be wish-making, nor bargaining—but truly trusting Jesus and praying to God according to His will. God is faithful, and He will surely accomplish it for us according to His word.

A Five-Point Summary and Encouragement

Let me briefly summarize today’s content: First, you must truly follow Jesus and put it into practice in your lifestyle—this is life. Second, you must pray according to God’s will. Third, you must pray to God with confidence and faith. Fourth, you must pray—not just mumble words. Fifth, you must not fail to pray, and you must not ask wrongly.

In the lesson on prayer in the one-on-one discipleship course, there is a passage that is very well said. Let us read it together as mutual encouragement.

God does not require us to have great faith. We only need to simply trust this great and true God. Only those who obey can experience the life of faith. No one has ever been able to have faith while disobeying the God who commands him to have faith! Because of faith, God will answer our prayers.
