Introduction: The Real Story of Losing Weight and Regaining It

On April 9 this year at 8:01 p.m., I weighed 99.05 kg—close to 200 jin. Sometimes cute children would even pat my belly, so I made up my mind to fight obesity to the end.

From baking my own whole-wheat bread, to boxing along with the TV, to running on the treadmill at the gym—in about two months, by 6:26 p.m. on May 31, my weight had dropped to 88.95 kg.

Then I relaxed again—no more boxing, no more exercise, no more bread-making. By Friday at 10:30 p.m., I was back to 96.5 kg.

When the pastor’s wife was fighting cancer, my weight reached 95 kg. Later, by postponing breakfast and moving dinner earlier, I lost weight all the way down to 79–80 kg. And as everyone knows, every time I stopped dieting, I gained it all back on my own merit.

I deeply feel that obesity is like the kind of stubborn unclean spirit Jesus spoke of in the Bible—it refuses to leave a person.

Regaining the weight twice made me realize that losing weight is like learning—like rowing against the current: if you do not press forward, you fall back. There are many ways to lose weight, but only persistent effort keeps the pounds from coming back.

Fat, Thin, and Strong Spiritual Lives

In fact, the growth of a Christian’s spiritual life is very similar to losing weight—it is easy to relapse. If Christians do not persist in following God and His word, their spiritual lives easily fall backward.

Our spiritual lives can be strong, thin, or obese. The strong are those who consistently hear and practice the word; the thin are those who hear and practice intermittently; the obese are those who only hear the word but never practice it.

Pay Much Closer Attention, Lest We Drift Away

Scripture · Hebrews 2:1-2

Therefore we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away from it. For since the message declared by angels proved to be reliable, and every transgression or disobedience received a just retribution,

Scripture · Hebrews 2:3-4

how shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation? It was declared at first by the Lord, and it was attested to us by those who heard, while God also bore witness by signs and wonders and various miracles and by gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed according to his will.

The author of Hebrews warns us that if we do not treat God’s word with increasing seriousness, we may drift with the current. Here “pay much closer attention” means: to focus on, to heed, to devote oneself to. When you hear the word of God, you need to listen carefully, focus on God’s word, and practice its teaching in your daily life.

The Warning Against Neglecting Salvation

But if you hear God’s word and treat it carelessly, you can easily ignore it; and when we ignore God’s word and neglect His salvation, we are guilty. What is stressed here is a fact, not condemnation.

We say that if you do not listen carefully to God’s word, do not focus on it, and do not practice its teaching, you may neglect this salvation. This is not condemnation, but it is a warning to Christians.

It is not a warning to those who do not know God, but to everyone who knows God—and especially to those who think they know God. Let us see what Hebrews 10:38-39 says.

The Righteous Shall Live by Faith

Scripture · Hebrews 10:38-39

But my righteous one shall live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him. But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.

The author of Hebrews quotes Habakkuk: “The righteous shall live by faith.” Believing in Jesus as Savior and Christ means more than just believing—it means acting to follow Jesus and His word. Faith plus the works that faith produces is living faith; faith without works is dead faith.

If at the time of believing you were willing to follow God’s word, but later you shrank back, the author of Hebrews says God takes no pleasure in a Christian who shrinks back—and shrinking back can lead to destruction. Let us look at Ezekiel 33:13.

Shrinking Back and Repentance: The Warning of Ezekiel

Scripture · Ezekiel 33:13

When I say to the righteous that he shall surely live, and if he trusts in his righteousness and commits injustice, none of his righteous deeds shall be remembered, but in his injustice that he has committed he shall die.

The righteous cannot rely on their past righteousness. When we sin, our past righteousness is no longer remembered by God, and we pay the price for the sins we have committed. If a sinner never repents until death, they die in their iniquity and are destroyed. This is rowing against the current—if you do not press forward, you fall back.

Scripture · Ezekiel 33:14-16

Again, when I say to the wicked, ‘You shall surely die,’ if he turns from his sin and does what is just and right, if the wicked restores the pledge, gives back what he has taken by robbery, and walks in the statutes of life, not doing injustice, he shall surely live; he shall not die. None of the sins that he has committed shall be remembered against him. He has done what is just and right; he shall surely live.

And when a sinner repents, turns away from his sin, and turns to God to do what is right, his past sins will not be remembered, and he will surely live in the righteousness of God.

Following God in Every Generation

Christian life differs a little from generation to generation, but God’s truth is continually practiced and applied. Every generation follows God on the foundation of the previous one. For a period before the Reformation, Christians rarely even had the opportunity to read the Bible themselves, because the church used Latin Bibles that only a few clergy could read.

As a result, the church of that time hardly understood justification by faith. It was not until the Reformation of Martin Luther—when the printing press in Heidelberg enabled the mass printing of Bibles and the doctrine of justification by faith was lifted high—that the church began to be released from a faith that overemphasized works.

Nothing should be carried to extremes. Emphasizing works while forgetting faith that relies on God is one extreme; emphasizing faith alone while ignoring the actions of faith is the other extreme. Only faith with works is the faith of justification.

In the 20th century, the Chinese church rarely stood to worship God; the basic pattern was call-and-response singing—the platform sang a line, the congregation repeated it, and finally everyone sang it through together. Entering the 21st century, churches began to worship God with new styles of praise and worship.

When you begin to follow God by entering into His heart for this generation, you are like one sailing with the current—your spiritual life grows quickly. Of course, in every generation, Christians need to submit to God’s word and follow it for their lives to keep growing.

Closing: Against the Current and With the Current

Following God’s word and submitting to Him, life keeps rising, rising, rising. If we do not submit to God’s word, no matter what church forms or trends we chase, life will not grow. So what I want to say is this: a Christian’s spiritual life is like rowing against the current—if you do not press forward, you fall back. And when a Christian begins to be willing to follow God and enters into God’s heart and current, that is sailing with the current. May we all be Christians who submit to God’s word and enter into God’s heart. Amen!
