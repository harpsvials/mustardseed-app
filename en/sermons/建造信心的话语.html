Introduction

Faith, simply put, is our confidence in something. For a Christian, faith from God is confidence in what? The Christian’s faith is confidence in God! Faith in God also includes faith in God’s word, for God’s word is the final standard by which we know God.

Our feelings and experiences can confirm that God’s word is true; but our feelings can never replace or deny God’s word. Human feelings are subjective perceptions—different people feel differently about the same thing, and each person’s feelings change over time. Let God’s word guide you; do not define God by your feelings.

If God is the only truth, then it is not your practice that tests whether He is truth; rather, His word corrects your behavior. Human traditions and theology can err—you may verify them against your knowledge of God’s word and your experience—but God’s word can always guide our lives. That is why the Bible, after thousands of years of human history, remains the world’s best-selling book.

I. The source of faith: God’s word

The source of faith is God, and God uses His word—the Scriptures—to anchor our faith.

Scripture · Romans 10:17

So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ.

The Greek behind "faith" is pronounced "pistis"—firm belief in and loyalty to the truth, or to God. In short, it is "faith." That is, faith comes from hearing. The word "hearing" is pronounced "akoe." Faith in and loyalty to God come from hearing certain things. So if a Christian wants to grow fervent for God, you need to hear. Hear what? The word of Christ. The Greek word here for "word" is "rhema," emphasizing the action of speaking.

This concept was first proposed by the ancient Greek philosopher Plato, who divided words into "onoma" (nouns) and "rhema" (verbs). Aristotle and later the Prague School developed this idea further, making it one of the foundations of modern linguistics.

Taken together, our faith comes from hearing Christ speak into our lives. How do we understand Christ speaking to us? In a narrow sense, Christ speaks to you face to face; but from a biblical perspective it is not only face to face, because Christ is now interceding for us at the right hand of the Father.

II. How to encounter God’s word in daily life

So how do we experience God’s rhema in real life? First, as we meditate and chew on God’s word, we sense God’s guidance through the Scripture in the present moment. Second, in worshipping God in spirit and in truth, we sense God speaking to our lives. Third, while listening to preaching on Sunday or at special meetings, we sense that God is speaking to us through the preacher. Fourth, other ways, including dreams or hearing God’s voice supernaturally and seeing visions God gives.

Whatever way God uses to speak to our lives, it builds our faith. But words must also be tested. In church we often hear people say that God told them something. They should test it before sharing it with brothers and sisters or with pastors. For example, a brother once told a sister he liked, "God said you are to marry me."

How should one respond to such a statement? If you really hear something like this, you need to test it and wait. When God truly leads you into a mature relationship and into marriage, then you can share it. But if you spread it everywhere, whether or not God actually spoke, you put yourself and others in an awkward position.

Mary in the Bible always treasured God’s revelations in her heart. When the shepherds found Mary and Joseph and passed on the angel’s words—that a Savior, Christ the Lord, had been born in the city of David, and the sign would be a baby wrapped in swaddling cloths lying in a manger—many who heard were amazed, but Mary treasured up all these things in her heart (Luke 2). Mary did not go around telling everyone and demanding things for her son.

Using a word you alone claim to have heard from God to demand things from others truly repels people! The Bible teaches us to obey. Outside of Scripture, if you really hear something, learn to wait and test; if it involves others, you also need to give them the opportunity to test whether your word is from God. But if it is already a clear teaching of Scripture, do not say, "Let me pray and test it."

For special revelations, give yourself and others room to test; for the teachings of Scripture, learn to obey and follow, and do not use "testing" as an excuse to resist God’s word.

III. rhema and logos

We say the word God is speaking to us now is "rhema"—it carries power and builds our faith! In church you will also hear the word "logos," which also refers to God’s word. Let us look at a passage.

Scripture · Matthew 8:8

But the centurion replied, "Lord, I am not worthy to have you come under my roof, but only say the word, and my servant will be healed."

In Matthew 8:8, the Greek behind "the word" is "logos"—a spoken word, a promise, or a declaration. Some say rhema is the word being spoken now, carrying power, while logos is the word already spoken and not as good. In fact, all of God’s word carries power!

rhema is the guidance you receive from God in the present; logos is the word and principles God has spoken. When God is speaking now, it is rhema; when you look back at God’s rhema, that word has already become logos.

These Greek terms can be confusing. That is why Greece produced so many great philosophers. Do not get tangled up in words: God’s rhema has power to build our faith, and God’s logos could immediately heal the centurion’s servant. The key is to receive, accept, and obey—because when you submit to God’s word, it has power to heal and set you free, to build your life and establish your faith!

rhema can become logos, and logos can become rhema. A word God speaks to you, if you do not follow it, becomes a memory of the past; a word recorded in the Bible for many years, if you follow it, can become rhema breathing life into you.

IV. How to test the word you receive

How do we test whether what we receive is really God’s word to us? Convictions outside of Scripture, or those that clearly contradict God’s word, are not the faith Scripture speaks of. A conviction that contradicts the Bible may be a good expectation that comes from man.

Your expectation may be good, but if you do not believe God’s word—that is, if you merely expect the promises in Scripture to happen to you—if you only wish for them and hope for them, however beautiful your hope is, hope and expectation are not faith.

Only when you believe with your whole being and whole heart does God’s word become rhema that builds your faith. Treating God’s word half-heartedly will never build our faith.

V. What does whole-hearted believing mean?

Faith does not merely interact with us in our thoughts. Faith lives in our minds, but not only in our minds. You cannot merely wish for good things to happen in your life! Rather, toward God’s word you must confirm with your reason that it is true, hope with your emotions that it will surely be fulfilled in your life, and submit with your will and follow it.

Reason, emotion, and will all submitting to God’s word—these three are all indispensable.

You cannot merely hope with your emotions that things will happen according to God’s word. Without rational understanding and willing obedience, your emotional hope is only wishing. Like a sick person saying, "I really hope I get healed!"—that cannot be called faith.

Nor can you only know in your mind that God is our healer, yet feel no hope in your heart, depressed as if you were about to die. Can you call that faith?

Nor can you only say, "Obedience brings blessing. I will follow God! I will keep the Lord’s Day! I will give!" Without rational knowledge of God and emotional hope, your obedience to God’s word becomes dry. It feels like your spiritual life is dying, and you seem to lose interest in the things of God.

But if you have obedience of the will toward God, added to rational knowledge of God, plus experiencing God’s reality, then emotional hope is kindled. Whatever circumstances you face, you believe God is in control, and the end will surely be different. Then you can love God and submit to Him and love Him all the more. Amen!
