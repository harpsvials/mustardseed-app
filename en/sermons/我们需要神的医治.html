Why Preach on Healing

Why preach on healing again? Although we have experienced much healing, we still have sickness and pain. Recently in church I have heard different brothers and sisters speak of pain in their backs and legs—this is the main reason for preaching on healing.

When brothers and sisters speak of back and leg pain, others are kind enough to give advice. But from the church’s perspective, we should speak like Peter in the Bible: “In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk (be healed).”

The Background of the Beautiful Gate

Scripture · Acts 3:1-10

Now Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. And a man lame from birth was being carried, whom they laid daily at the gate of the temple called the Beautiful Gate to ask alms of those entering the temple. Seeing Peter and John about to go into the temple, he asked to receive alms. And Peter directed his gaze at him, as did John, and said, “Look at us.” And he fixed his attention on them, expecting to receive something from them. But Peter said, “I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk.” And he took him by the right hand and raised him up, and immediately his feet and ankles were made strong. And leaping up he stood and began to walk, and entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God. And all the people saw him walking and praising God, and recognized him as the one who sat at the Beautiful Gate of the temple asking for alms, and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.

According to the biblical background notes (see The New Testament Background Commentary), this story took place around three in the afternoon—the “ninth hour” in the text—when people were preparing to go to the temple to pray. The Jews of that time had three daily times of prayer at the temple.

This story happened exactly as Peter and John were going to the temple to pray. The “Beautiful Gate” is said to be a nickname for the Nicanor Gate (named after the Alexandrian donor). (Reference: The New Testament Background Commentary.)

It was the main and largest gate, cast from the most expensive bronze. It stood in the court of women on the east side, facing the sanctuary, and must have been especially beautiful at sunrise. Fifteen steps led up to it, and women, the disabled, and the unclean could not pass through it. Many beggars probably sat on the steps, begging from those entering the temple courts of Israel. (Reference: The New Testament Background Commentary.)

For these vulnerable people of that time, begging at the temple gate was their way of making a living, and giving alms was a respected act in their society, so many people were usually willing to give them money.

The disabled man in the text, lame from birth, was carried every day to this “Beautiful Gate,” where the most people passed by. The background notes mention the place was especially beautiful at sunrise; today, everyone would take photos and check in there—back then, everyone liked to pass through this gate.

Asking and Looking Intently

Now that you basically understand the background, this disabled man came to “ask” Peter and John to give him money. The word “asked” is not a lowly word—for Jesus speaking with the disciples, and the high priest “questioning” Jesus, use the same word.

Interestingly, the Bible’s word for “asking/praying” is the same word. I want to say: when you pray, you are not begging Jesus to give you alms—you are speaking with God according to His word.

Of course, we do not pray the way a modern beggar begs, nor are we arrogantly demanding that God do things for us—we pray to God according to His word.

When the disabled beggar saw Peter and John, he asked them for money. As you know, before following Jesus, Peter and John were fishermen—and from the biblical account, they were not rich at all. So when someone came asking, they “looked intently” at the beggar.

“Looked intently” in the original text means “to gaze at, to stare at.” I imagine Peter and John were rather puzzled why someone would beg from them—that is why they stared at the beggar.

Peter said, “Look at us.” The text says the man fixed his attention on them, expecting to receive something from them. Is it possible that the beggar, seeing Peter and John’s shabby clothes, held little hope of receiving anything great—yet still had a faint hope of getting something from them?

Back to Peter: the lame man was sizing up Peter and his companion, hoping for some benefit. But Peter’s purpose was for this man to look at him—not to see whether Peter had money to give, but to fix his gaze on Peter.

If someone asks you for what you do not have, give them what you do have. And you have Jesus—you can give Jesus to those who ask something of you.

I Have No Silver and Gold

Just as the disabled man was gazing at Peter, Peter suddenly declared loudly, “I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk!”

Peter’s words caught the disabled beggar completely off guard. The beggar’s original thought was simply: you give money or you do not. He never expected Peter to say, in Jesus’ name, rise up and walk!—and then be pulled up by the hand.

Immediately Made Strong

When the beggar was pulled up, his feet and legs immediately began to be made strong! When you begin to be blessed, healing should also come immediately! Do not say, “My illness is too serious!” Say, “Immediately made strong!” Amen!

No matter what our feelings tell us, we must believe that when we receive a healing prayer, healing is happening! Amen! Your feelings run slower than your faith! And when you give birth to the faith for healing, your feelings will then flood over your body. It is the feeling of healing returning to your body through your faith!

When the disabled man was healed, he leapt up praising God! Or stood there! Or walked around letting those passing through the Beautiful Gate see what God had done in him! Amen! He no longer chose to sit there!

Sometimes we kindly say, “Your body just got better—how can you jump and stand for so long? Sit down and rest, or you will feel unwell again!” Brothers and sisters, let us stop binding people with negative declarations!

I will begin to rejoice over what God has done! Our mouths must declare positive and life-giving words—not negative words!

Declaring Positive Words

Scripture · Romans 10:10

For with the heart one believes and is justified, and with the mouth one confesses and is saved.

Your mouth can declare the salvation you believe in your heart, and your mouth can also declare the healing you believe. When your mouth declares the salvation you believe and confess, you receive the salvation you believe! In the same way, when your mouth declares the healing your heart believes, healing begins to manifest in your body!

Do not say, “I will believe only when I see it.” Instead, because God’s word says, “By his wounds you have been healed,” believe that because of Jesus’ wounds, I have been healed! Amen!

Scripture · 1 Peter 2:24

He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed.
