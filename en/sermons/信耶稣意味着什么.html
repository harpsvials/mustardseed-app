Introduction

A young man saw Mark Twain reading the Bible and was puzzled. In the young man's impression, Mark Twain always liked to joke about religion and had even written many words satirizing the church. The young man could not help walking over and asking, “Mr. Twain, how is it that a man so skeptical of religion like you is reading the Bible? Don't the parts you can't make sense of or understand trouble and distress you?”

Mark Twain put down the book, took off his glasses, looked at the young man, and answered with his signature dry humor: “Young man, what truly troubles me is not the parts of the Bible I can't understand; on the contrary, it is the parts I understand all too well.”

“If Christ were here now there is one thing he would not be a Christian.”——Mark Twain, Following the Equator (1897)

Mark Twain often satirized the hypocrisy of Christians—reading the Bible yet not living it out. It was not only so in his day; Christians today must also be careful: are we reading the Bible yet not living it?

The Faith of the Early Church

Let us first compare the background of believing in Jesus in the early church with that of the church today. Our altar-call prayer today actually differs in meaning from that of the early church. In the early church, the disciples knew that believing in Jesus could mean losing their lives, while today the cost of believing in Jesus is very small.

The faith of the early church disciples was considered blasphemy against God by Judaism. The Jews had been taught since the time of Moses that there must be no other gods besides God, and when Israel was enticed to worship the golden calf and the gods of the nations, God disciplined them severely.

Israel's Historical Background

Later, in the days of the judges, because they worshipped God on one hand and idols on the other, some tribes nearly perished. Then, in the time of Solomon, the kingdom of Israel reached its height—and it was in this period that King Solomon took one thousand foreign concubines and built pagan temples for them. After Solomon, Israel was immediately split into two kingdoms.

During the period of the two kingdoms, the kings of the northern kingdom of Israel were mostly idolaters, while the southern kingdom was the legitimate heir of the dynasty of David and Solomon; some of its kings feared God, while others followed foreign customs and worshipped idols. In the end, both kingdoms were conquered by foreign powers.

Finally, over the course of hundreds of years, the people of Israel gradually returned to Israel from foreign lands. Even today, Israel still has the Aliyah, which brings Jewish people back to Israel from all over the world.

The Aliyah

In 1948–1949, driven by waves of anti-Semitism and persecution, about 45,000 Yemenite Jews sold their property and crossed hundreds of miles of desert on foot or by donkey to temporary refugee camps in British-controlled Aden. Many suffered robbery, hunger, and disease along the way.

At the end of 1948, Israel, together with American and British pilots and civilian airlines, used nearly 40 passenger and cargo planes for a secret airlift. Because the surrounding Arab states closed their airspace, the planes had to fly long, dangerous routes over the Red Sea and along hostile borders.

From December 1948 to September 1950, the operation carried out more than 380 flights. Most Yemenite Jews had never seen modern machinery; they regarded the wings of the airplanes as the “great eagle” of biblical prophecy sent to save them, and boarded them in peace.

On the planes they recited Exodus 19:4, thanking God for delivering them from the persecution of the nations according to the Scriptures.

Bible · Exodus 19:4

You yourselves have seen what I did to Egypt, and how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself.

They arrived safely—an entire community returned. In total, the operation safely brought back about 49,000 Yemenite Jews, nearly bringing the entire Jewish community of thousands of years in Yemen back to the land of Israel.

Looking back: Israel was conquered and exiled many times in history, and returned many times, experiencing countless sufferings along the way. And the root of all this suffering is that they left God and worshipped idols.

Why the Religious Leaders Persecuted Jesus

So when the triune God became flesh and came into the world, beyond the understanding of Israel, Jesus also had power to heal the sick and cast out demons. He had great influence, and vast numbers followed Him.

The religious leaders of that time were deeply afraid that Israel would once again fall into worshipping other gods and be disciplined by God. Another reason was that the religious leaders feared Jesus threatened their position of authority.

Under the combination of these factors, the religious leaders of Israel joined with the Roman ruling class in persecuting Christians. Believers discovered in those days would basically be arrested, killed, or thrown into the arena.

Giving Your Life to Jesus

So why do we say that believing in Jesus in the early church differed from today's Christians making an altar-call prayer? Because back then, believing in Jesus basically meant giving your life to Jesus—giving everything to Jesus. By comparison, today when a Christian makes an altar-call prayer, he does not have to pay much of a cost.

This is also why the early Christians strictly kept the teachings of Jesus Christ and of the apostles. They regarded their faith as more important than life itself—a situation similar to that of Christians in the Middle East, Christians in North Korea, and Christians in China during the Cultural Revolution.

Today, if we truly want to follow Jesus and be His disciples, we must revisit the historical background of the early church and be willing to take up the heritage of faith passed down from the apostles.

Judas's Disappointment

The faith of Jesus' disciple Judas was actually like this. Many people at that time, including Judas, hoped Jesus would lead them on the road of national restoration.

But before going to the cross, Jesus repeatedly taught the disciples that He would be arrested and would suffer. To Judas, and to many believers who expected Jesus to lead Israel to restoration, Jesus seemed undoubtedly too weak—no longer fit for them to follow.

So Judas was deeply disappointed with Jesus, which led to the event of Judas betraying Jesus. Of course, behind it was also the work of the devil and Satan. We often say, flies do not land on an egg with no crack. Had Judas's expectation of Jesus not exceeded his faith in Jesus, he would not have let Satan enter his heart and work within him.

The Source of Faith

Today, if we do not first put other people and things ahead of Jesus and our faith, Satan cannot disturb our faith within us. Many times it is not our circumstances that drive us away from God, but whether the source of our faith is Jesus Christ—that is what determines whether we are choked by thorns.

The Cost and the Harvest

People who do not yet believe often ask: does believing in Jesus cost money? Questions about money are not easy to answer. You ask whether believing in Jesus costs money; in fact, believing in Jesus is about whether you will give your life.

If we are unwilling to offer our lives to Jesus, then when hard times come, or when our faith faces pressure, it will be cast aside at any moment.

Of course, if you are unwilling to pay the cost to follow God on earth, one day—when Jesus comes, or when we go to heaven—our condition will not be very good either.

But if on earth you exchange the temporary for the eternal, and exchange what must be lost for what can never be lost, your condition in heaven will be very good. After all, if you acknowledge Jesus on earth, Jesus will also acknowledge you in heaven.

May we all faithfully follow Jesus and His word, becoming people who pay the cost for Jesus and reap a hundredfold.
